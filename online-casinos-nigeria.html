How It Works

Until late 2024, Nigeria's gambling industry operated under the National Lottery Act 2005, administered federally by the NLRC, alongside separate state-level regimes such as Lagos State's. This dual system created years of jurisdictional conflict. The Supreme Court resolved it decisively: lotteries, betting and gaming fall under the residual powers of the states, not the federal government. The NLA was declared ultra vires and unenforceable outside the FCT — it was not formally repealed by Parliament — and the NLRC it created has de facto ceased regulating gaming in the 36 states (a separate FCT Lottery Regulatory Office was created for Abuja in May 2025).

In practice, this means an operator must be licensed by the specific state(s) it wants to serve. Lagos State's Lotteries and Gaming Authority (LSLGA) is the most established and widely referenced regulator, licensing casinos, sports betting, gaming machines and promotions. More than 20 states have since set up or activated their own gaming authorities. To avoid forcing operators to collect a separate license in every state, a coalition calling itself the Federation of State Gaming Regulators of Nigeria (FSGRN) introduced a reciprocity framework — a license from one participating state is honored by other member states. This is a voluntary, state-driven arrangement, not a federal regulator, and it only covers states that have joined it. In May 2025 the coalition formalized this as the Subnational Reciprocity Licensing Framework, issuing a Universal Reciprocity Certificate (URC) to qualifying operators.

A late-2025 attempt to reverse this — the Central Gaming Bill, which would have created a federal gaming commission on the theory that online gaming runs over federally-regulated telecoms networks — was introduced on 7 October 2025 and cleared both chambers of the National Assembly on 2 December 2025, but President Bola Tinubu publicly stated he would not sign it. The bill is not in force as of September 2026 (a two-thirds legislative override of the veto remains theoretically possible). As of this writing, the state-by-state system stands.

FAQ

Is online gambling legal in Nigeria?

Yes, for adults 18+, but there is no longer one national license. Since the Supreme Court's November 2024 ruling, each state regulates gambling separately; look for a license from the state a site is registered in (e.g. Lagos LSLGA) or from a state participating in the FSGRN reciprocity framework.

What happened to the NLRC?

The National Lottery Regulatory Commission lost its legal footing when the Supreme Court declared the National Lottery Act 2005 ultra vires and unenforceable in all states except the Federal Capital Territory. It has de facto ceased licensing or regulating gaming operators in the 36 states, though Parliament has not formally abolished it.

What is FSGRN?

The Federation of State Gaming Regulators of Nigeria is a voluntary coalition formed by several state gaming regulators (not a federal body) that created a reciprocity system: an operator licensed by one member state can operate in other member states under that single license. It does not cover states outside the coalition.
